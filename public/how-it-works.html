Catan Lens answers one question: where should I put my first two settlements? It scores every legal corner with a small set of rules that strong players agree on and explains the result in plain sentences. Same board, same answer: there is no machine learning and nothing leaves your browser.

The rules in one table

RuleWhat it means at the table
ProductionAdd the dots on the surrounding number tokens: a 6 or 8 has 5, a 2 or 12 has 1. They count the ways two dice roll that number, out of 36. Production dominates the score.
Resource valueWheat and ore build cities and development cards, so they count a little more; sheep a little less. Later seats lean toward wood and brick.
ScarcityA resource that is rare on this board is worth more.
PairsWood with brick, or wheat with ore, from the same corner earns a small bonus.
RepeatsTwo hexes with the same number is feast-or-famine: a small penalty.
Harbours3:1 helps anyone; 2:1 only if you produce plenty of that resource.
Room to growA strong open corner two roads away is worth something; a dead end is not.
Second settlementCredit for covering what the first one lacks, especially wheat and ore.
Starting roadPoint it at the best corner two roads away that the other seats are not likely to take before your next turn.

Every rule traces to a published source. The source table, the exact weights and what was left out are in RESEARCH.md; the full explanation is in HOW-IT-WORKS.md.

Does following it win games?

In thousands of complete four-player games played by Catanatron's strong bot, one seat had its opening chosen by different strategies and then played on like everyone else. A fair seat wins 25%.

These are bot games, so they show the advice is sound, not how much it will raise your own win rate. Details and the code to reproduce them are in the repository.
