How Catan Lens works

← Back to the app

Catan Lens answers one question: where should I put my first two settlements? It scores every legal corner with a small set of rules that strong players agree on and explains the result in plain sentences. Same board, same answer: scoring runs in your browser, with no account or analysis service. Links contain the board data you open or share.

If you opened this page from the app, Back to the app keeps the board and placements carried in your link, even after refreshing this page.

Use the keyboard

Settlement advice waits until every hex has a resource or desert and every resource hex has a number token. Then finish the standard harbour set, choose Ignore harbour effects, or confirm a custom set with Use these … harbours. Excluding harbours keeps your entered coast intact while removing harbour effects from every recommendation. Overlapping ports must be repaired or excluded. Removing a tile or token hides advice until it is repaired; your placed pieces stay on the board.

In Your board, choose a paint, Numbers or Ports, then Tab to a hex or coastal edge and press Enter or Space. The number picker focuses the current token; Tab to another token and press Enter to set it. Escape returns to the hex. Your place stays focused after each edit.

Changing between the 19- and 30-hex layouts starts a blank board of the new shape. Restore previous board or the browser's Back button recovers the board you had before, including its placements, even after a refresh. Start blank, Random board and leaving Your board for Explore keep the same recovery in this tab. A copied link shares only the current board.

In Explore, New board, a changed seed and the balanced-numbers switch also keep the previous board. Clear removes settlement marks and offers Restore cleared pieces, with the original placement order. Clearing an empty board or entering the same seed keeps the useful recovery point.

An unreadable board link shows an error and keeps its original data in the address bar. Advice stays hidden until you recover the board or explicitly start again. If the saved board matches the other board size, the app offers the matching player counts so you can recover its tiles, harbours and settlements.

In Drill, Tab to an unlocked level and press Enter or Space to replay it. Locked levels become available as you clear the ladder.

Scores show hundredths, and score percentages round down so only an exact tie displays 100%. Drill misses show the point gap; a close model score can still describe a good real-game choice. The exercise checks the app's ranking, and score shares are not winning chances.

Drill progress is stored in this browser, separately for each table. If reading or saving fails, a warning offers Retry saving. You can keep practising in this tab, but unsaved progress may be lost if you reload or close it. Retrying preserves other tables' saved progress.

Leaving Drill returns to your previous board, settings, placements and placement tool, including after a refresh during setup or an exercise. That return point stays with this tab's current history entry. A copied link shares the Drill table setup or exercise; it does not include the board you were exploring before it.

Understand your opening

After your second settlement, Your opening shows average cards per 36 rolls, the chance of any income and the five-resource breakdown. Repeated numbers can pay several cards together without improving that chance. These averages assume available resources and omit the robber and trades; actual rolls vary.

Missing-resource notes connect your production to the things you can build. The summary also lists your starting hand and owned-harbour trade rates. Plan your first builds shows the extra cards each purchase would need from that hand; each row is a separate option, and building still needs a legal place or purchase on your turn. Which rolls pay? lists every dice total.

When previewing a second corner, open What this opening gives you to see the same figures before Keep. This preview stays hidden during blind practice and unanswered drills. Confirming a choice focuses its result and brings it into view on a phone.

Ignoring harbour effects also removes their trade rates from the summary. Cities & Knights figures are labelled as a two-settlement comparison: the app does not model its starting city or commodities, so it withholds base-game income and build planning in that mode.

The rules in one table

RuleWhat it means at the table
ProductionAdd the dots on the surrounding number tokens: a 6 or 8 has 5, a 2 or 12 has 1. They count the ways two dice roll that number, out of 36. Production dominates the score.
Resource valueWheat and ore build cities and development cards, so they count a little more; sheep a little less. Later seats lean toward wood and brick.
ScarcityA resource that is rare on this board is worth more.
PairsWood with brick, or wheat with ore, from the same corner earns a small bonus.
RepeatsTwo hexes with the same number is feast-or-famine: a small penalty.
HarboursWhen included, 3:1 helps anyone; 2:1 helps most when you produce plenty of that resource. A harbour type you already own adds no duplicate bonus. A nearby harbour needs two roads and a new settlement before use.
Room to growA strong open corner two roads away is worth something; a dead end is not.
Second settlementCredit for covering what the first one lacks, especially wheat and ore.
Starting roadPoint it at the best corner two roads away that the other seats are not likely to take before your next turn.

Every rule traces to a published source. The source table, the exact weights and what was left out are in RESEARCH.md; the full explanation is in HOW-IT-WORKS.md.

Does following it win games?

The current version avoids rewarding a harbour type you already own. Its comparison is separate from the earlier scoring policy's results below; no weights were tuned on these outcomes.

Catanatron's value-function bot plays every seat. One seat's opening is chosen by each strategy, then it plays on like everyone else. Strategies face the same boards and seat orders. An evenly matched seat would win 25%.

Owned-harbour correction · 9 October 2026

This separate test finished 24,000 games on another 8,000 fresh boards. Both policies were fixed before their games began. The correction removes credit for trading access you already have; it was not chosen to improve these results.

The correction had four fewer wins than the older policy: 1,972 versus 1,976 out of 8,000. That is −0.05 percentage points, with the planned approximate 95% interval from −0.098 to −0.002. Only four win/loss pairs changed, all against the correction, so the narrow estimate rests on very few changed outcomes. This test shows no win-rate gain from the correction and does not prove equal performance.

Against the bot's own opening, the corrected policy's difference was −0.16 percentage points (95% interval: −1.67 to +1.35). That comparison remains uncertain.

Earlier policy on fresh boards · 9 October 2026

This test used 40,000 complete games on fresh board and bot tie-breaking seeds. The scoring policy was fixed before the test; these outcomes were not used to tune it.

Across matched runs, Lens's advantage over “most pips” was 7.9 percentage points, with a 95% interval from 6.5 to 9.3 points. Against the bot's own opening, the difference was −0.2 points, with an interval from −1.8 to +1.3. That comparison shows no clear advantage and does not prove equal performance.

These results cover bot play in the four-player base game. They describe the frozen policy tested in that setting; they do not predict your personal win rate or validate three-, five- or six-player games, Cities & Knights, or play against people.
