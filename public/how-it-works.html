Catan Lens answers one question: where should I put my first two settlements? It scores every legal corner with a small set of rules that strong players agree on and explains the result in plain sentences. Same board, same answer: there is no machine learning and nothing leaves your browser.

Use the keyboard

Settlement advice waits until every hex has a resource or desert and every resource hex has a number token. Then finish the standard harbour set, choose Ignore harbour effects, or confirm a custom set with Use these … harbours. Excluding harbours keeps your entered coast intact while removing harbour effects from every recommendation. Overlapping ports must be repaired or excluded. Removing a tile or token hides advice until it is repaired; your placed pieces stay on the board.

In Your board, choose a paint, Numbers or Ports, then Tab to a hex or coastal edge and press Enter or Space. The number picker focuses the current token; Tab to another token and press Enter to set it. Escape returns to the hex. Your place stays focused after each edit.

Changing between the 19- and 30-hex layouts starts a blank board of the new shape. Restore previous board or the browser's Back button recovers the board you had before, including its placements, even after a refresh. Start blank, Random board and leaving Your board for Explore keep the same recovery in this tab. A copied link shares only the current board.

An unreadable board link shows an error and keeps its original data in the address bar. Advice stays hidden until you recover the board or explicitly start again. If the saved board matches the other board size, the app offers the matching player counts so you can recover its tiles, harbours and settlements.

In Drill, Tab to an unlocked level and press Enter or Space to replay it. Locked levels become available as you clear the ladder.

Drill progress is stored in this browser, separately for each table. If reading or saving fails, a warning offers Retry saving. You can keep practising in this tab, but unsaved progress may be lost if you reload or close it. Retrying preserves other tables' saved progress.

Leaving Drill returns to your previous board, settings and placements within the current page session. A drill link shares that drill; it does not include the board you were exploring before it.

The rules in one table

RuleWhat it means at the table
ProductionAdd the dots on the surrounding number tokens: a 6 or 8 has 5, a 2 or 12 has 1. They count the ways two dice roll that number, out of 36. Production dominates the score.
Resource valueWheat and ore build cities and development cards, so they count a little more; sheep a little less. Later seats lean toward wood and brick.
ScarcityA resource that is rare on this board is worth more.
PairsWood with brick, or wheat with ore, from the same corner earns a small bonus.
RepeatsTwo hexes with the same number is feast-or-famine: a small penalty.
HarboursWhen included, 3:1 helps anyone; 2:1 helps most when you produce plenty of that resource. A nearby harbour needs two roads and a new settlement before use.
Room to growA strong open corner two roads away is worth something; a dead end is not.
Second settlementCredit for covering what the first one lacks, especially wheat and ore.
Starting roadPoint it at the best corner two roads away that the other seats are not likely to take before your next turn.

Every rule traces to a published source. The source table, the exact weights and what was left out are in RESEARCH.md; the full explanation is in HOW-IT-WORKS.md.

Does following it win games?

In thousands of complete four-player games played by Catanatron's strong bot, one seat had its opening chosen by different strategies and then played on like everyone else. A fair seat wins 25%.

These are bot games, so they show the advice is sound, not how much it will raise your own win rate. Details and the code to reproduce them are in the repository.
