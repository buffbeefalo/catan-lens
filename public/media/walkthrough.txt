How does Catan Lens choose? It scores every legal corner with a handful of rules that strong players agree on, and production comes first. Every number token carries dots. A six or an eight has five; a two or a twelve has just one. The dots count the ways two dice can roll that number, out of thirty-six. A corner collects from up to three hexes, so its production is the sum of their dots. Wheat and ore count a little extra, because they build cities and development cards. Resources that are rare on this board are worth more. Smaller bonuses reward wood with brick, wheat with ore, a harbour you can actually feed, and room to grow. Repeating a number costs a little. Every rule traces back to a published source: expert guides, champion interviews, and a statistical study of four hundred online games. Placement is a snake draft. Projected placements plays it out for every seat, so you can see what is likely to be gone before your turn comes back. The suggested road steers away from corners other players will probably take first. Keep a first settlement, then choose your second. Your opening separates expected cards from how often you collect. Repeated numbers can pay several cards together, and the resource breakdown shows what you produce. The summary shows your starting hand and harbour trade rates. Open Plan your first builds to compare that hand with the cost of each purchase. Each row is a separate option, before new rolls or trades. Drill mode turns this into practice. First, set the table: how many players, and whether to use Cities and Knights. That option changes resource weights; it does not simulate cities or commodities. Then climb sixteen levels with hints hidden until you commit. Miss, and you see your rank, the score gap and what the level needed, then get a fresh board at the same level. Find the best corner, and the level is cleared. Later levels demand the exact best score on near ties. A closely scored choice can still be sensible in a real game. Playing at a real table? Enter the board in front of you. Pick a resource and tap hexes to paint them, choose Numbers to set the tokens, and tap the coast to place harbours. Advice waits until the tiles are complete and the harbours are checked. Finish or confirm the harbours you entered, or choose Ignore harbour effects to leave ports out of the analysis.
