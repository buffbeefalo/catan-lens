How does Catan Lens choose? It scores every legal corner with a handful of rules that strong players agree on, and production comes first. Every number token carries dots. A six or an eight has five; a two or a twelve has just one. The dots count the ways two dice can roll that number, out of thirty-six. A corner collects from up to three hexes, so its production is the sum of their dots. Wheat and ore count a little extra, because they build cities and development cards. Resources that are rare on this board are worth more. Smaller bonuses reward wood with brick, wheat with ore, a harbour you can actually feed, and room to grow. Repeating a number costs a little. Every rule traces back to a published source: expert guides, champion interviews, and a statistical study of four hundred online games. Placement is a snake draft. Projected placements plays it out for every seat, so you can see what is likely to be gone before your turn comes back. The suggested road steers away from corners other players will probably take first. Drill mode turns all of this into practice. First, set the table: how many players, and whether you play Cities and Knights. Then climb sixteen levels, easy to hard, with hints hidden until you commit. Miss, and you see your rank and what the level needed, then get a fresh board at the same level. Find the best corner, and the level is cleared. Later levels demand the exact best corner on near-ties, from any seat, and for your second settlement too. Playing at a real table? Copy your board in a minute. Pick a resource and tap hexes to paint them, choose Numbers to set the tokens, and tap the coast to place harbours. Every ranking now applies to your board, and Copy link saves the whole thing, so you can share it with your table. But does the advice actually win? In forty thousand simulated games between strong bots, openings chosen by Catan Lens won about as often as the bots' own searched openings, and far more often than simply grabbing the most dots.
